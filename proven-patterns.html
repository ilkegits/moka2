Proven app patterns, built into ShiCards

Layouts and habits that top learning apps use, each with the evidence behind it and a free source you can open. Collected on 18 November 2026 from Duolingo's own blog, RevenueCat, Adapty, Superwall, Calm, Nielsen Norman Group and published studies. No paid libraries were used.

Almost every number comes from a big app or a company that sells app tools. Treat them as a direction, not a promise for a small app.

Onboarding 入门

实

A real lesson before anything else

No account, no sign-up wall. Onboarding only finds your level. There is still no sign-up wall: after the check you go straight into the app, and your first round is your first lesson.

Duolingo moved sign-up behind the first lesson: about +20% daily users.

First Round Review

Lesson kept out of onboarding on purpose; no sign-up wall
实

You pick the daily goal, nothing pre-picked

Four goals in minutes (5, 10, 15, 20). Each shows how soon you finish your level at that pace. The button stays off until you choose and says “Commit to my goal”.

At Duolingo, making people choose and the word “Commit” instead of “Continue” were both wins. Pre-selecting the harder goal lost.

Lenny's Podcast, Duolingo streak lead

In the app: onboarding, Streak page
常

Why you're learning, your level, then a placement check

Pick as many reasons as you like, then Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced or Pro. Up to four rounds of 12 characters move up or down until they find your HSK level, which sets where Hanzi of the day and new words start.

Duolingo uses this order and offers a placement test so learners skip what they know. Only ask what changes the app, and say why.

Appcues GoodUX · NN/g

In the app: onboarding pages 2 to 5
研

A progress bar that starts fast

The bar jumps on the first steps and slows near the end.

A study of 32 experiments: steady bars didn't help; fast-then-slow bars cut drop-off, slow starts made it worse.

City, University of London

In the app: top of every onboarding page
研

A head start

The characters you tap in the check go straight into your word bank as known, so you never start from zero.

Loyalty cards with 2 free stamps were finished 34% vs 19% of the time.

Nunes & Drèze, 2006

In the app: character check
实

Ask for the reminder after the first win

Right after you choose your daily goal: pick a time and tap “Remind me”, or “Not now”. On phones without reminders, it offers Add to home screen instead.

Calm: people who set a reminder stayed about 3× as often; asking after the first session got 40% to set one.

Amplitude on Calm · WebKit: web push on iPhone

In the app: last onboarding page
研

Hints on the spot, not a slide tour

“Tap every one you know” sits on the character check itself. No swipe-through welcome slides.

NN/g's test: tutorial slides didn't help, and made tasks feel harder.

NN/g, mobile tutorials

In the app: character check

Streak and habit 习惯

实

Daily goal kept apart from the streak

One round keeps the streak. The goal is extra: a green ring around the flame fills as you practise.

Duolingo split the two: +3.3% day-14 retention, +40% more people on 7-day streaks.

Duolingo blog

In the app: flame on Decks, Streak page, end of round
实

“Streak saved” page

After a freeze covers a missed day, a full page shows the ice-blue flame, the week, and freezes left.

Free freezes were “one of the biggest wins”; two beat one, three taught people to skip.

Lenny's Podcast · Duolingo blog

In the app: replaces the old small message
研

Win a lost streak back, free

“Practise 3 days in a row this week and it comes back.” Three bars fill as you go.

Duolingo's free Earn Back was a “major retention win”. Seven studies: a broken streak hurts less when it can be repaired.

Silverman & Barasch, 2023

In the app: welcome back page, Streak page
常

Welcome back, gently

After 3+ days away: Shi Shi cheering, “Life happens”, and a 5-card warm-up instead of the full pile.

Returning users are about 20% less likely to stick; the first 3–4 days after a lapse are the best time to win them back.

Duolingo blog

In the app: shows once on your first visit back
常

Milestone celebrations

A full page at 3, 7, 14, 30, 50, 100, 150, 200, 365 days, with a Share button.

Retention jumps most from day 1 to 3 and flattens after day 7, so the early milestones matter most.

Deconstructor of Fun

In the app: shows once per milestone
实

Badges for real skills

Nine gold seals on Profile, each with its own progress bar: words, streaks, writing, stories, HSK 1.

Duolingo's badges: +2.4% daily users. A badge for just signing up did nothing.

In the app: Profile

A dot on the app icon

The installed app's icon shows a dot until today's practice is done.

Duolingo's red dot: +6% daily users, then +1.6% more.

Empty states that point somewhere

With nothing to review, Learn says “All caught up” with a Read button instead of nothing.

NN/g: say why it's empty and give a direct way forward.

End of round with tomorrow's preview

Cheer, what you remembered, your streak, today's goal, and “Tomorrow: 3 cards”.

The Hooked model's last step: set up the next visit.

Reading 阅读

研

A story says how much of it you can read

The cover shows “You know 12 of 17 words” with a bar, and new stories pick their new words near your own HSK level.

Knowing 95% of a text's words is the least for adequate reading; 98% is best. Later studies argue about the exact number, so it is a guide, not a rule.

Laufer & Ravenhorst-Kalovski, 2010

In the app: Read, the story cover
研

A shelf that fills, with the count in sight

Every finished story is a book on a shelf of 20. “4 of 20” sits beside it, and the story you are on is named. A full shelf goes on: a story read again turns its book gold.

People speed up as a reward gets closer: café customers bought coffee 20% faster as they neared the free one.

Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng, 2006

In the app: Read, Your shelf
实

The streak moves on right after the story

The first story of the day ends on a page with the new book, “day 5” and the week's ticks. The cover says a story keeps the streak.

Duolingo's new animation after extending a streak: new learners 1.7% more likely to still be there a week later.

Duolingo blog

In the app: Read, after “I finished this story”
研

The same words, again and again

Stories are written with words from your word bank, and every book on the shelf can be read again.

After one easy book, readers kept about 1 new word in 25 three months on, and none they had met fewer than 8 times. Words need many meetings.

Waring & Takaki, 2003

In the app: Read, every story
研

A quick check before the story is done

“I finished this story” first asks what each new word of the story means, with three answers each. A wrong answer shows the right one and you carry on.

Students who took one test on a text recalled 56% of it a week later; those who read it again, 42%. Minutes after reading, re-reading was still ahead: the gain shows up later. Their test was retelling a text, not picking a word's meaning.

Roediger & Karpicke, 2006

In the app: Read, after “I finished this story”

Map 地图

研

The goal stands on the map

The monument you are saving for is a ghost on its spot, with Shi Shi over it and a gold ring on the ground that closes as coins come in. The map opens by gliding down to it.

Goal-gradient effect: people speed up as a reward gets nearer, and visible progress makes them come back sooner. Coffee-card holders bought faster with each stamp.

Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng, 2006

In the app: Map
研

Show the smaller number

The saving bar reads “220 / 400” before halfway and “180 to go” after it.

Small-area hypothesis: effort rises when attention is on whichever is smaller, what is done or what is left.

Koo & Fishbach, 2012

In the app: Map, Shop, monument pages
研

You pick your own goal

Every monument has a price tag on its real spot. Tap one and “Save for it” moves Shi Shi there; tags turn gold when you can pay.

Self-determination theory: a goal people choose themselves holds motivation better than one handed to them.

Ryan & Deci, 2000

In the app: Map
研

Today's coins, every visit

A small gold tag on the saving bar: “+60 today”.

The progress principle: of everything that lifts motivation on a given day, seeing even a small step forward does the most.

Amabile & Kramer, HBR 2011

In the app: Map

Paywall 付费

实

“How your free trial works” timeline

Today: everything unlocks. Day 5: we remind you. Day 7: you pay, cancel any time before.

Blinkist: +23% conversion, −55% complaints. Adapty: +12–36% revenue per user in winning tests. One test won trial starts but lost on retention.

RevenueCat · Adapty · Clearful test

Yearly first, priced per month

Two plans. Yearly is picked first with “SAVE 50%” and $2.50/month; Monthly below at full price.

Yearly-first layouts: +25–40% revenue per user. Picking yearly first didn't lower sales.

Close and “Not now” in plain sight

An X in the corner from the first second, and a “Not now” link under the button. The price sits right under the trial wording.

Hidden or delayed close buttons and trial text louder than the price get apps rejected.

Leagues and leaderboards

Weekly leagues against other learners.

Strong at Duolingo (+17% learning time) but needs accounts and a server. Fake opponents would be a dark pattern.

Countdown timers, guilt-trip copy, hidden close

“Offer ends in 09:59”, “No, I don't want to learn”.

Home-screen widgets and store rating prompts

Duolingo's widget users retain far better.

A web app can't make phone widgets or ask for a store rating. The icon dot and Add to home screen stand in.
